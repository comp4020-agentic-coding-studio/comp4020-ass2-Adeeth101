## Biological efficiency

    BE = fresh mushroom mass ÷ dry substrate mass × 100 %

| Substrate | Published BE range |
|---|---|
| Straw | ~60–80 % |
| Sawdust | 13–61 % |
| Supplemented | ~100 % |

BE can exceed 100 % because mushrooms are mostly water. **It is not a
thermodynamic efficiency.**

*These figures come from search summaries rather than papers read in full. They
carry a medium confidence label, and nothing banded depends on them.*

---

## Species set the months

- *Pleurotus ostreatus* — about 13–24 °C
- Blue oyster — about 7–18 °C
- Pink and golden oysters — about 18–30 °C

Between the cool and warm species you can cover roughly **8–30 °C** of monthly
mean temperature.

---

## Worked example: Brisbane

Monthly mean 14.9 to 25.0 °C, so **some oyster species fruits in all twelve
months** with no heating or cooling.

    20 kg dry straw × 0.70 BE = 14 kg fresh mushrooms
    14 kg × 5 = 70 kg fresh spent substrate

At USDA composition figures (33 kcal and 3.31 g protein per 100 g):

    14 kg → 4,620 kcal and 463 g protein
         → 0.13 % of energy, 0.7 % of protein

A tenfold scale-up still leaves you under 2 % of energy.

---

## So what is it for?

**The substrate leaves heavier than it arrived.** About 5 kg fresh spent
substrate per kg picked, near 2.65 % nitrogen on a dry basis, C:N about 13.

That is a genuinely good soil input — and a genuinely bad insect feed.

> Black soldier fly larvae convert pure spent mushroom substrate at **1.46 %**.
> Effectively nothing. Draw the arrow to **compost and soil**, weeks 2 and 10.

Brisbane's real constraint this week is strata rules, not biology.
