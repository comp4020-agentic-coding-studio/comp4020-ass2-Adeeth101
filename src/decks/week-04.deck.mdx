## Thresholds and dry matter

Black soldier fly larvae have a **lower developmental threshold of 10.4–12.3 °C**.
Below it, development stops.

Egg to adult: 91 days at 19 °C, 42 at 28 °C, 32 at 31 °C. Best population growth
near 30 °C.

This course screens each site with a linear productive factor ramping from 12 °C
to 27 °C — **a simplification, and labelled one.**

On food waste, larvae achieve about 52 % substrate reduction and **28 %
bioconversion** over 14 days on a dry-matter basis. **This course uses 20 %**, as
a conservative course assumption.

## Worked example: Darwin, the best site there is

Productive factor 0.97 — thermally productive essentially all year.

| Step | Factor | Running total |
|---|---|---|
| Household food waste | — | 380 kg/yr |
| Plant-based share (course rule) | × 0.60 | 228 kg/yr |
| Dry matter | × 0.25 | 57 kg DM/yr |
| Bioconversion | × 0.20 | 11.4 kg DM/yr |
| Darwin thermal factor | × 0.97 | **11.1 kg DM/yr** |

Demand side — a 4 m² grow bed at the FAO feed rate:

    50 g/m² × 4 m² × 365 d = 73 kg feed/year
    11.1 ÷ (73 × 0.9) = 17 %

---

## Seventeen per cent. At the best site.

With the whole household's plant-based scraps committed.

- An **8 m² bed halves it** to about 8 %
- **Canberra outdoors**, at a thermal factor of 0.19, manages about 3 %

The other 83 % is bought feed. Under this course's closure definition, **fish
grown on bought feed are bought fish** — so a Darwin excellent design counts
0.27 kg of fish protein a year against a household requirement of 67.5 kg.

That is a result, not a reason to abandon the module.
