## FAO Technical Paper 589

| Rule | Figure | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Feed rate, leafy greens | 40–50 g/m²/day | p. 17, §2.4.2 |
| Feed rate, fruiting vegetables | 50–80 g/m²/day | p. 17, §2.4.2 |
| Maximum stocking density | **20 kg per 1,000 L** | p. 109, §7.3.3 |
| Daily water loss | 1–3 % of system volume | p. 29, §3.4 |

That last line matters more than it looks: a 1 m³ system loses 10–30 L a day.
The course carries a **20 L/day** placeholder and sizes it properly in weeks 7–9.

**Aquaponics is not where your water goes.**

## Worked example: Adelaide

Monthly mean 11.3 to 22.7 °C. No single legal species covers that, so Adelaide
needs a **rotation** — harvest and restock, not one continuous stock.

A 4 m² bed at 50 g/m²/day for twelve months:

    50 × 4 × 365 = 73 kg feed/year
    73 ÷ FCR 2.0  = 36.5 kg live fish
    × 40 % fillet × 20 % protein = 2.9 kg protein

Apply the closure rule — Adelaide's larvae cover 9 % of the ration:

    2.9 kg × 0.09 = 0.26 kg counted, against 67.5 kg required

## What the model cannot see

The screening model ranks by temperature alone. For Canberra it returns **Murray
cod and rainbow trout, seven months**.

Murray cod is precisely what an ACT household may not keep without a licence.

---

## The instrument, not the forum post

The *Nature Conservation Act 2014* (ACT) excludes fish from "animal" **unless**
the fish has special protection status. Murray cod is EPBC-listed; silver perch
is ACT-vulnerable and EPBC critically endangered.

Importing **any** live fish into the ACT needs a licence or written approval.

In Queensland, SA and the NT the definitions turn on **sale**, which is why those
answers are shorter. Redclaw crayfish is **noxious in South Australia**.
