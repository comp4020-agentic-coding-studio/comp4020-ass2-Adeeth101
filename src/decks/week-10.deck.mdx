## Balance by stream, in dry matter

Every stream tracked separately, in kg of **dry matter**, destination named.

**Waste-stream recovery is deliberately not banded.** It has no reference
calculation behind it, and publishing a band without one would be inventing a
number. It is marked from your balances against the rubric instead.

> **In equals out plus losses.** A balance that closes because you wrote the gap
> into a residual line has not closed. Name the loss and say what process causes
> it: respiration, leachate, volatilisation.

---

## Hot composting: the target

The Australian composting standard as described by a state environment regulator:

- core above **55 °C for three consecutive days after each of at least three turns**
- manure, food and grease-trap feedstocks: **fifteen consecutive days after each of at least five turns**
- whole process not less than six weeks

---

## Two scope notes you must carry

1. That is a **commercial windrow standard**. No household is bound by it — this course uses it as the target a bay is *designed against*
2. It is more demanding than usually quoted: fifteen days after **each** of five turns, not fifteen in total

WHO's separate figure for composting excreta is above **50 °C for at least one
week** — and WHO cautions it is "mainly recommended as an off-site secondary
treatment at large scale".

---

## Anaerobic digestion, and the biogas rule

Strongly temperature dependent — screening factors run from **0.63 at Darwin to
0.07 at Canberra**.

**Biogas earns you nothing.** It is energy, it belongs to the proposed sequel, and
only the digestate's nutrients count here.

**Record it anyway.** An unrecorded output is the same mistake as an unrecorded
import.

---

## Worked example: Alice Springs

90 days a year at or above 35 °C. Reaching 55 °C is a question of managing
**moisture**, not of adding heat.

| Stream | Mass | Destination |
|---|---|---|
| Plant-based food waste | 228 kg/yr | Insect unit, then frass to soil |
| Meat, dairy, cooked | 152 kg/yr | Hot compost or digester — 15-day class |
| Spent substrate | ~5 kg per kg picked | Compost and soil |
| Fish sludge | from the system | Compost or digester; never larvae |
| Garden prunings | site-dependent | Mushroom substrate, then compost |

---

## Alice Springs' trap

A bay that dries out stops composting and **never reaches pasteurisation
temperature**.

So the water hot composting consumes is a genuine line item on the site's most
constrained resource.

It is easy to write a compost plan here that quietly needs water the site does
not have.
