# What crosses the fence line

**SLOP4761** · Week 1

Designing the Closed-Loop Household I

---

{/* _class: quote */}

A household is not self-sufficient because it produces everything.

It is self-sufficient when the output of one system is the input to another.

---

## So we are not closing the loop

We are measuring **how much of it closes**.

And being equally precise about what still comes through the gate.

## Three consequences

1. Carting water in **lowers** your score. It does not vanish.
2. Fish fed on bought pellets are **bought fish**.
3. A bigger garden can never lower your water score.

That third one was a bug we had to fix.

---

## Why not just divide production by demand?

It can exceed 100 %.

So it rewards growing food nobody eats.

And it cannot see a single imported kilogram.

---

## Today's calculation

Roof runoff, enHealth Appendix B, after Martin (1980):

**runoff (L) = A × (rainfall − B) × roof area**

A = 0.80 · B = 2 mm per month

---

## Canberra

300 m² of roof. 616 mm of rain.

`0.80 × (616 − 24) × 300 = 142,080 L`

**142 kL**

Household demand: **219 kL**
