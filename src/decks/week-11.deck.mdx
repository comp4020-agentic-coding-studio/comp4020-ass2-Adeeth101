# The nitrogen has nowhere to go

**SLOP4761** · Week 11 · Sanitation, nutrient limits and choosing

---

## This week's question

The household excretes **13.6 kg of nitrogen a year**, about 88 % of it in urine.

A garden absorbs roughly **12 g of nitrogen per m² per year** before the surplus
becomes a pollution problem rather than a fertility one.

**Multiply those out for your site. What do you do with the rest?**

---

## By the end you can

1. Build an uptake-limited nitrogen balance and find a destination for the surplus
2. Size an excreta chamber to your site's WHO storage band and say why the band moves
3. Compare an existing condition with three alternatives and mark the dominated ones

---

## An uptake-limited balance

    Absorbable N = production area (m²) × 0.012 kg N/m²/yr

| Site | Growing area | Absorbable N | Share of 13.6 kg |
|---|---|---|---|
| Darwin | 500 m² | 6.0 kg | **44 %** |
| Canberra | 450 m² | 5.4 kg | 40 % |
| Alice Springs | 400 m² | 4.8 kg | 35 % |
| Adelaide | 250 m² | 3.0 kg | 22 % |
| Brisbane | 130 m² | 1.6 kg | **11 %** |

Between **56 % and 89 %** of the household's nitrogen has to go somewhere that is
not your garden.

**A design problem, not a design failure.**

---

## Storage, and why Canberra pays for being cold

WHO guidance on the safe use of excreta, Table 4.5 — dry excreta, no new material
added:

| Ambient temperature | Storage time |
|---|---|
| 2–20 °C | **1.5–2 years** |
| Above 20–35 °C | Over 1 year |
| Alkaline treatment | pH above 9 for over 6 months |

Canberra 13.1 °C and Adelaide 17.0 °C sit in the long band. Alice Springs,
Brisbane and Darwin need one year.

Urine, WHO Table 4.6: at 20 °C, one month for crops that will be processed; six
months for all crops.

---

## So Canberra pays for being cold

**About 75 % more chamber capacity than Darwin, for the same household.**

A climate-driven burden with nothing to do with how well the design is thought
out. The reference designs already absorb it, so the bands do too.

## Dominated, and cheapest feasible

A **dominated** option is worse on every row than another. Strike it out, with one
sentence of reason.

Then find the **cheapest option that meets your client's stated targets**.

Anything dearer has to justify itself in Assignment 2 — and this grid is where you
find out what it would have to justify.

## Your tutorial task

Part one, 45 minutes: chamber capacity from your band; the nitrogen balance; then
**find your jurisdiction's greywater instrument** and write two sentences — what
the law says, and where this course is stricter. Four of five sites will find the
course rule is not their law.

Part two, 45 minutes: the grid, the dominated options marked, the cheapest
feasible option identified.

**And say what you are declining**, with the number behind it. That is a result.
