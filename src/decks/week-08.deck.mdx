## This week's question

Roof water is not clean.

**What does each barrier in your treatment train actually remove, expressed as a

"Filtered" is not an answer. Neither is "we boil it if we are worried".

## A log reduction is an order of magnitude

1 log = 90 %. 2 logs = 99 %. 3 logs = 99.9 %. In series, logs add.

WHO rates household water treatment **separately for bacteria, viruses and
protozoa**, because a technology strong against one can be useless against
another:

| Rating | Bacteria | Viruses | Protozoa |
|---|---|---|---|
| Three star | ≥4 log | ≥5 log | ≥4 log |
| Two star | ≥2 log | ≥3 log | ≥2 log |

**A single "log reduction" figure with no class attached is not usable.**

---

## What the barriers actually do

- **First flush and leaf screen** — no log rating. It removes the load the rest of the train would otherwise carry
- **Sedimentation and cartridge pre-filter** — turbidity control. **UV's job is impossible through turbid water**

---

## The two that carry numbers

**BioSand filter** — about **2.5 log** for *E. coli* once mature. But **1.67 log**
run intermittently, against 3.71 log continuous, and a field average across
studies of about **1.1 log**.

**UV disinfection** — Class A delivers **≥40 mJ/cm² over lamp life**, with a
sensor and a flow restrictor. Lamps replaced every **9–12 months** — an operating
cost, not a capital one.

**The gap between laboratory and field is this week.**

---

## One scope correction

**No Australian retailer page reviewed for this course claimed NSF/ANSI 55
Class A.** Australian units cite **WaterMark**, which is a different scheme
answering a different question.

Do not write a Class A claim into a design you have not sourced.

---

## Worked example: Brisbane

Smallest roof in the set (170 m²), most intense rain (1,146 mm). First flush at
10 L per 50 m²:

    170 ÷ 50 × 10 L = 34 L diverted per event

Real water lost, and Brisbane can least afford it — divert less and the pre-filter
clogs faster and the UV sees higher turbidity. That is the trade.

Energy:

    UV lamp 25 W × 24 h = 0.60 kWh/day

**12 % of the 5 kWh/day allowance, running whether or not anyone draws water.**
The largest constant load in the reference design.
