# Policies and support

Rules that apply across the whole course, so that an assessment brief can link
here instead of restating them and getting one of them slightly wrong.

## Safety and scope

**This course designs systems. It does not build or operate them.**

Nothing in SLOP4761 asks you to construct, plumb, commission, eat from or drink
from anything. Every deliverable is a drawing, a calculation or a written
justification. If a studio activity ever appears to ask otherwise, it is a
mistake in the material and you should say so.

That boundary is deliberate. The course works with sanitation, water treatment
and fermentation, and each of those has a failure mode that hurts somebody. A
design exercise can be as rigorous as we like about them without putting a single
student at risk.

## Course design rules are not legal requirements

Several of this course's rules are **stricter than the law** at some or all of the
five sites, and in a few cases they are stricter than the law everywhere.

Wherever a rule appears on this site it carries its status: a *course design rule*
with its reasoning, or a *legal requirement* with the jurisdiction and the
instrument it comes from. Where the two differ, the page says so.

The obligation this puts on you is simple and it is assessed: **state what your own
jurisdiction actually requires, and cite the instrument you read.** Not a forum
post, not a supplier's page. Week 11's studio runs this as its main activity.

## Figures, labels and what you may rely on

Every number on this site is labelled with its basis:

| Label | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Sourced** | Taken from a named document, with a citation |
| **Course assumption** | Set by the course for assessment consistency |
| **Indicative cost** | From a retail listing, for the costing exercise only |
| **Model estimate** | An output of the course's screening model |
| **Synthetic data** | Generated for teaching. Not a measurement |

The screening model is not a validated engineering design, and its outputs are
not predictions of real yields. Reproducing its numbers is part of the work;
trusting them outside this course is not.

Where a figure could not be verified against a primary source, the page says
that too. Week 3's mushroom yields are the clearest example: they rest on search
summaries, they are labelled, and nothing that is banded depends on them.

## Assessment

Four items, totalling 100 %:

| Item | Weight | When |
|---|---|---|
| Five quizzes, 8 % each | 40 % | Weeks 3, 5, 8, 10 and 12 |
| Food Loop Design | 20 % | Friday of week 7 |
| Site-Specific Off-Grid Master Plan | 40 % | Assessment period |

There is **no oral defence**. The troubleshooting dataset, the calculations and the
written justification already test whether you understand your own design, and an
oral component would add a condition that is hard to apply equally to everyone.

### Quizzes

Conceptual multiple choice, on paper, at the start of the studio. Twenty minutes,
no calculators, no numeric working — if a question can be answered by arithmetic
it is not on the quiz.

They are spread so that keeping up is worth more than catching up, and none falls
in the week the Food Loop Design is due.

**This website does not collect or store quiz answers.** It is a static site with
no server behind it. The practice questions on each quiz page reveal their own
answers in the browser for your use only; nothing is recorded, marked or
transmitted.

### Performance bands are not grades

The [reference bands](/method/) place your reported indicators against your own
site's thresholds. They are **evidence inside the marking criteria** — they inform
*Calculations* and *Loop integration* — and nothing converts a band into a mark.

A band 2 design with honest assumptions and a closed balance marks above a band 3
design whose numbers cannot be reproduced.

The bands are recomputed and republished whenever a course constant changes. If
that happens mid-semester, the affected thresholds are announced and no work
already submitted is re-marked against new ones.

## Late work and extensions

Work is due at the time stated on its brief. Ask for an extension **before** the
deadline and it is routine; ask afterwards and it is not.

Where an extension is granted, the new date is the date. Where circumstances go
beyond what an extension covers, use the university's own process rather than
negotiating with the course.

## Academic integrity

The usual rule: the work you submit is yours, and anything that assisted you is
declared.

Two things worth saying specifically for this course.

**Declare your tools.** Spreadsheets, scripts and generative tools are all fine and
several are expected. Say what you used and for what. A model that computed your
tank balance is a method; an unstated one is a problem.

**Assumptions are where the marks are, so make them yours.** Two students on the
same preset will produce similar parameter sheets, because the preset is the same.
They should not produce the same assumptions, the same uncertainty list or the
same account of what fails first. Those are the parts being assessed.

## If you think a band or a mark is wrong

Bands first: they are computed by a published method from a published model, so if
one looks wrong it is checkable. Bring the numbers to the convenor and we will
recompute in front of you. This has happened, and the model has been wrong before.

Marks: raise it with the marker first, then the convenor. A re-mark is a fresh
read by a different marker, and the new mark stands whether it is higher or lower.

## Getting help

- **Design questions** go to your studio. The answer is usually useful to more than
  one person in the room.
- **Course-level questions** — the price schedule, a client target that looks
  unreachable, a threshold that looks wrong — go to the convenor.
- **Choosing a home.** There are five fixed client homes and no others; the
  choice is made in week 1 from the [clients page](/clients/).

Accessibility adjustments are arranged through the university's process, and the
course accommodates whatever that produces without needing to know why.
