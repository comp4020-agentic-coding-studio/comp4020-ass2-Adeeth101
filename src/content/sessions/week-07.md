## What this tutorial is for

You know what the garden wants and what the household wants. Today you work out
what the site actually produces, month by month — and you do the final pass on
Assignment 1, which is due at 5 pm on Friday.

**Story stage.** The hinge. **Assignment 1 is due at the end of this week.** Monday's
lecture previewed what a measurement year at your home produced; the full
specialist handover follows the deadline. Nothing you were shown on Monday changes
what your register should say, and nothing you compute today is an observation of
this house.

## The words this week uses

- **Runoff coefficient** — the fraction of rain landing on a roof that reaches the
  tank. Not 1.0; the course uses 0.8 unless you argue otherwise.
- **Initial loss** — rain absorbed by the roof and gutter before anything runs. The
  enHealth method deducts about 2 mm per event.
- **First flush** — the first volume off the roof, diverted because it carries the
  dirt. Sized by a stated rule, in litres per square metre of roof.
- **Supply curve** — twelve monthly volumes in kL reaching storage. The mirror of
  week 2's demand curve.

## Do this

**1. Validate before you compute (15 minutes).** Put 750 mm and 200 m² into your
sheet and confirm it returns **116,160 L**. Only then point it at your own site. A
formula that reproduces the published worked example is a formula you can defend;
one that does not is a formula you are about to build a tank on.

**2. Then the twelve rows (30 minutes).** Applying the formula to an annual total
instead of month by month is the error that survives longest, because the answer
looks plausible. It overstates a dry site badly: Alice Springs has months where
rainfall is below the 2 mm initial loss and the correct contribution is **zero, not
negative**.

Deduct the first flush, and name the rule you sized it by.

**3. Then the overlay (20 minutes).** Supply curve against demand curve, same axis,
both in kilolitres. Most students see the gap for the first time here, and it is
usually wider than they expected and in a different month.

**4. Then the last pass on Assignment 1 (25 minutes).** Run week 6's two
cross-checks again on the register: every row points at a decision, every decision
has at least one row. Then check the total against $1,500 and write the headroom
down. Ask the tutor about anything in the brief you are unsure of — this is the
last session before it is due.

## The output

**A twelve-row monthly supply curve** for your site, in kilolitres, with the first
flush deducted and every source named, laid beside week 2's demand curve. Plus a
measurement register ready to submit.

## Check your own before you submit

1. Does your sheet return 116,160 L for the published worked example?
2. Is runoff computed **per month**, never from the annual total?
3. Is any monthly contribution negative? It should be floored at zero.
4. Is the first-flush deduction there, with its sizing rule named?
5. Are supply and demand on the same axis in the same units?
6. Did you use the **connected** or guttered roof area rather than the roof plan
   area?
7. Is Assignment 1 finished, costed and ready for Friday?
