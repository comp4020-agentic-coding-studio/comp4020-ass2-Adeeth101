## What this tutorial is for

Two things, and the second is the bigger one. First, size preservation — the
storage term between a harvest curve and a flat consumption curve. Then assemble
the measurement register, which **is** Assignment 1.

**Story stage.** The last scoping week. Assignment 1 is due at 5 pm on the Friday
of week 7, so this is the session where it comes together and next week's is the
last chance to fix it.

### If you missed earlier weeks

You can still do the second half, which is the assessed part and the part that
becomes your assignment. For the first half use **PRACTICE — Wattle Street, a
harvest curve peaking at 30 kg in March against a flat consumption of 8 kg a
month.** Invented round numbers for teaching.

## The words this week uses

- **Harvest curve** — your twelve-row monthly yield, from week 2's planted area.
- **Consumption curve** — flat, because households eat at a steady rate.
- **Surplus** — the area above the consumption line. **Deficit** — the area below
  it. Storage is sized on the **smaller** of the two: you cannot store more than
  you grew, and you gain nothing storing more than you will eat.
- **Measurement register** — sheet 3 of your workbook. One row per measurement,
  ten columns, no blanks.

## Do this — part one, preservation (35 minutes)

**1. Draw both curves on one axis.** Harvest by month, consumption flat.

**2. Size the store** in kilograms and litres, and give it a floor area. A capacity
with no floor area is not sited, and week 12 will ask where it went.

**3. Then the losses.** Nobody has a sourced number for trimming, fermentation and
spoilage, and the course does not supply one. State yours, say where it came from,
and be prepared to defend the **order of magnitude** rather than the digit.

**4. Then the energy.** Adelaide and Canberra students will find their bench sits in
band at ambient and their load is zero. Darwin and Alice Springs students will not,
and the control load they write down here is one of the few things that can push a
design over 5 kWh/day.

We do not make anything, and this course gives no general fermentation rule. If you
want to ferment at home, use a tested recipe under its own conditions; the lecture
explains why.

## Do this — part two, assembling Assignment 1 (55 minutes)

**5. List the decisions first.** Go back through weeks 1 to 5 and write the
*decisions*, not the measurements: connect the house roof or not; keep fish or not;
rear larvae indoors or outdoors; plant zone G3 or leave it lawn. Eight to twelve
decisions is typical.

**6. Then attach measurements to them.** Every `unknown` row on sheet 1, every
operating condition from week 5, every factor you took on faith from a lecture.
Fill sheet 3 completely — a row missing any column is an incomplete row:

| id | what_is_measured | decision_it_informs | method | location | units | frequency | accuracy_or_resolution | equipment_cost_aud | service_cost_aud |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| M01 | tank water level | whether the house roof is worth connecting | pressure level sensor, monthly manual dip as the check | existing 5 kL tank, garage roof | kL | hourly logged | ±3 % of full scale, about 150 L | 180 | 0 |

**7. Cross-check both ways.** Two passes, and both find something:

- Every **row** points at a decision. A measurement no decision depends on is money
  spent to fill a spreadsheet — delete it.
- Every **decision** has at least one row. A decision with nothing behind it is a
  guess wearing a number — add one, or say openly that you will settle it on
  published figures and why that is good enough.

**8. Total the cost.** Equipment plus services against the **$1,500** investigation
allowance. Your own hours are scheduled, not charged. State the total, state the
headroom, and write down what you dropped and why — the dropped list is marked.

## The output

**A sized preservation store** with its floor area and any control load, and a
**measurement register on sheet 3 with at least twelve complete rows**, costed
inside the allowance.

## Check your own before you submit

1. Is the store sized on the smaller of surplus and deficit?
2. Are the loss terms stated as assumptions with a reason, on sheet 4?
3. Is any temperature-control load in watts and hours, flagged for week 12?
4. Does every row on sheet 3 have all ten columns filled?
5. Does every decision on your list have at least one measurement row?
6. Does the programme total under $1,500, and is the headroom written down?
7. Have you written down what you dropped?
