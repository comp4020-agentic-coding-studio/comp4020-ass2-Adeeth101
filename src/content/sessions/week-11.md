## What this tutorial is for

Two halves. First the nitrogen, which is the constraint that caps every site.
Then the comparison itself — the grid that Assignment 2's recommendation comes
out of.

**Story stage.** Design, second last week. This is the last graded checkpoint.

## Open these first

- **Your workbook**, week 2's planted area and week 10's balance.
- **[Week 11's lecture](/lectures/week-11/)** for the uptake-limited balance and the
  storage bands.
- **[The price schedule](/method/costing/)** — the grid is costed from it.
- **Your home's jurisdiction** on its [dossier](/clients/).

### If you missed week 10

You need a planted area and a rough nutrient input. Use **PRACTICE — Wattle Street:
120 m² planted, receiving 4 kg of nitrogen a year from compost.** Invented round
numbers for teaching.

## The words this week uses

- **Storage band** — the WHO guidance period that excreta must be stored before use,
  which depends on climate: warmer sites need less time.
- **Uptake limit** — how much nitrogen a garden can absorb per square metre per year
  before the surplus becomes pollution rather than fertility. The course uses
  **12 g N/m²/year**.
- **Greywater** — wastewater from showers, basins and laundry, but not toilets.
- **Dominated option** — an option that is worse than another on every axis you care
  about. It can be struck out without further argument, and marking it is part of
  the exercise.

## Do this — part one, nitrogen (45 minutes)

**1. Size the chamber.** Storage band from your site's annual mean, then capacity.
Canberra and Adelaide students will find they need roughly 75 % more volume than the
warm sites for the same four people, which is worth sitting with.

**2. Then the nitrogen.** Absorbable N against the household's **13.6 kg a year**.
Everybody's surplus is large. The activity is deciding where it goes — extended
storage, alkaline treatment, export to a larger area, or reduced recovery in the
first place — and costing that decision.

**3. Then the citation exercise**, which is the point of the session. Find your
jurisdiction's greywater instrument. Read what it actually requires. Write two
sentences: what the law says, and where this course is stricter. Four of the five
sites will find the course rule is not their law.

## Do this — part two, the options grid (45 minutes)

**4. Write the existing condition down first.** From your findings page: what the
home currently uses, imports and wastes. Every option is compared against this, and
"do nothing" is a legitimate baseline that sometimes wins on one axis.

**5. Build three options.** Coherent packages, not shopping lists, and all three
actually buildable on your site. One should be noticeably cheaper than the others —
that is usually the interesting one.

| measure | existing | option A | option B | option C |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| cost, $ incl. 10 % contingency | 0 | | | |
| peak-month energy, kWh/day | | | | |
| water closure / garden water satisfaction | | | | |
| client targets met | | | | |
| maintenance, h/week | | | | |

**6. Mark the dominated options.** An option worse on every row than another is
struck out, with a line through it and one sentence of reason. Then identify the
**cheapest option that meets your client's stated targets**. Anything more expensive
than that has to justify itself in Assignment 2, and this grid is where you find out
what it would have to justify.

**7. Say what you are declining.** If mushrooms, insects or aquaponics do not earn
their place on your site, write the sentence now, with the number behind it. That is
a result, not a gap.

## The output

**A nitrogen balance with a destination for the surplus**, a cited greywater
instrument, and an **options grid** with the dominated options marked and the
cheapest feasible option identified.

## Check your own before you submit

1. Does your chamber capacity match your site's band, and can you say why the band
   differs?
2. Does the nitrogen balance show absorbed and surplus separately, with the surplus
   going somewhere?
3. Have you cited an actual instrument, and said where the course rule is stricter?
4. Does the grid include the **existing condition** as a column?
5. Are all three options buildable on your site, and is one of them clearly cheaper?
6. Are dominated options marked, each with a one-sentence reason?
7. Is the cheapest option that meets the client's targets identified?
