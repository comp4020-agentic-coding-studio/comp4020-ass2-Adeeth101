## What this tutorial is for

"Filtered" is not an answer. Today you build a multi-barrier train where every box
carries a number somebody else could check, and you find out what running it costs.

**Story stage.** First design week. The specialist findings are in hand, so the
water you are treating is the water the measurement year actually found at your
home — turbidity, *E. coli*, hardness, pH, and the sample taken after heavy rain.

## Open these first

- **Your home's specialist findings** — the water-quality table on your client's
  findings page, reachable from [clients](/clients/). Look at the post-rain sample
  before you design anything.
- **[Week 8's lecture](/lectures/week-08/)** for the barrier table and the log
  reduction figures.
- **[The price schedule](/method/costing/)** for what each barrier costs and what it
  does not price.

### If you missed week 7

You do not need week 7's supply curve today. You do need your home's findings page,
which is published and does not depend on anything you have done.

## The words this week uses

- **Barrier** — one treatment stage: a screen, a first-flush diverter, a sediment
  filter, a BioSand filter, a UV lamp. Each removes some pathogens and not others.
- **Log reduction** — an order of magnitude removed. 1 log removes 90 %, 2 logs 99 %,
  3 logs 99.9 %. Logs across barriers in series add.
- **Pathogen class** — bacteria, viruses and protozoa behave differently. A barrier
  strong against one may do almost nothing to another, so a single figure for "log
  reduction" with no class attached is not usable.
- **Continuous versus intermittent load** — a UV lamp that runs whenever the pump
  might run is continuous; a pump that runs twenty minutes a day is intermittent.
  They are totalled differently.

## Do this

**1. Quiz 3 (20 minutes).** On paper, on weeks 6 to 8.

**2. Read your own water first (10 minutes).** From your findings page: what is the
turbidity after heavy rain, and did any sample show *E. coli*? Write those two
numbers at the top of your sheet. They are what the train has to deal with, and a
train designed before looking at them is usually inadequate.

**3. Build the train left to right (25 minutes)** and put a number on each box, by
pathogen class, with its source. **The box you cannot find a number for is the
interesting one** — mark it *unquantified* and say what you would need to measure.
Marking it unquantified is a correct answer; inventing a figure is not.

| barrier | bacteria | viruses | protozoa | source | note |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| first-flush diverter | — | — | — | course rule | reduces load, not a credited barrier |
| sediment filter | unquantified | unquantified | unquantified | — | turbidity removal only |
| BioSand filter | see lecture | see lecture | see lecture | lecture | field average differs from laboratory |
| UV lamp | see lecture | see lecture | see lecture | lecture | requires low turbidity to work at all |

**4. Then the field-versus-laboratory exercise (15 minutes).** A BioSand filter rates
2.5 log mature, 3.71 log run continuously, 1.67 log run intermittently, and about
1.1 log as a field average. Decide which figure applies to a household that draws
water in bursts, and **write down why**. That sentence is the exercise.

**5. Then the certification check (10 minutes).** If you have written NSF/ANSI 55
Class A, find an Australian supplier page that claims it. Most will find WaterMark
instead, which is a different scheme answering a different question. Claim only what
your cited product actually holds.

**6. Total the energy (10 minutes).** Continuous and intermittent separately, then
summed, in kWh/day. The UV lamp alone is 0.6 kWh/day, continuous, and it is the load
most designs forget is always on.

## The output

**A treatment train drawn barrier by barrier**, each with its log reduction by
pathogen class and its source, plus the train's counted energy load split into
continuous and intermittent.

## Check your own before you submit

1. Are your home's own post-rain turbidity and *E. coli* figures at the top of the
   sheet?
2. Does every barrier carry a figure by pathogen class, or the word *unquantified*?
3. Where the source distinguishes laboratory from field, does your sheet?
4. Are continuous and intermittent loads separated, and then totalled?
5. Is every certification you name one your cited product actually holds?
6. Would the train actually work on your water, or does the UV sit behind something
   too turbid for it?
