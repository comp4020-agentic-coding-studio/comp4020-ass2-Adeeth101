## What this tutorial is for

The most-drawn arrow in permaculture is scraps to larvae to fish. Today you size
it and find out what fraction of the feed it really covers.

**Story stage.** Scoping. The 380 kg waste figure is a published per-household
convention, not a measurement of this house — which is exactly why it belongs on
your measurement register.

## Open these first

- **Your workbook**, sheet 1, for the household food-waste figure.
- **[Week 4's lecture](/lectures/week-04/)** for the threshold temperatures and the
  thermal factor for each site.
- **Your home's constraints** on its [dossier](/clients/) — some sites will not
  allow an outdoor larval unit where you would like to put it.

### If you missed week 3

You do not need week 3's output for this. You do need the household waste figure
from sheet 1: **380 kg a year**, the course convention for two adults and two
children.

## The words this week uses

- **BSF** — black soldier fly, *Hermetia illucens*. Larvae eat wet organic waste;
  the adults do not feed.
- **Bioconversion** — larval dry mass produced per unit of feed dry mass. This
  course works on a dry-matter basis throughout, so both sides are dry.
- **Thermal factor** — the fraction of the year your site is warm enough for larvae
  to develop. Their lower developmental threshold is 10.4 to 12.3 °C.
- **The import arrow** — the fish feed you buy because the loop does not close.

## Do this

**1. Run the chain (25 minutes).** Five multiplications, and everyone in the room
gets a different answer because of the last one:

| step | factor | source |
|---|---|---|
| household food waste | 380 kg/year | course convention, sheet 1 |
| plant-based share | 0.60 | course rule, lecture |
| dry matter | 0.25 | lecture |
| bioconversion | 0.20 | lecture |
| your site's thermal factor | from the lecture's five-sites table | lecture |

Write each factor on the sheet as its own row. A single multiplied answer with no
working is not an attempt.

**2. Then the choice that matters (20 minutes).** Rear outdoors and accept your
climate's factor, or rear indoors at about 20 °C and take roughly 0.53 everywhere
at the cost of a **counted heating load**. Canberra students effectively have no
choice. Darwin students should be able to say why they would decline. If you
choose indoors, the load goes on your sheet now, in watts and hours a day, ready
for week 12.

**3. Then draw the import (20 minutes).** Every diagram in the room gets an arrow
coming in from outside, labelled with a percentage of total fish feed. **If yours
does not, you have made an arithmetic error.**

**4. Route the rest (15 minutes).** Meat, dairy and fish sludge do not go to the
larvae in this course. Write the destination you are sending each to, and the
reason. The reason is a course design rule about pathogen and vector risk in an
uncontrolled household unit, and you should be able to say that rather than
"because the notes said so".

## The output

**A feed mass balance** with every factor as its own row, and the import line drawn
in and labelled with a percentage.

## Check your own before you submit

1. Are all five factors on the sheet, each with its source?
2. Is the thermal factor your **own** site's, or have you declared an indoor load
   instead?
3. If indoors: is the heating load in watts and hours, on the sheet, flagged for
   week 12?
4. Does your diagram show an arrow entering from outside with a number on it?
5. Are meat, dairy and sludge routed away with a reason written next to them?
6. Does your site's dossier actually permit the unit where you have drawn it?

## Where it goes next

The feed mass balance goes into week 5, where it caps how much of your fish counts
as yours.
