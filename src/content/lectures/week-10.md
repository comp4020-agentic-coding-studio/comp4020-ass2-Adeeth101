## The question

Your household generates 380 kg of food waste a year, your mushrooms leave five
kilograms of spent substrate for every kilogram picked, your fish tank produces
sludge, and weeks 4 and 5 sent the meat and dairy away unprocessed.

**Where does each of those go, and does the mass balance close?**

## The method: mass balance by stream, then check the temperature

Every stream is tracked separately, in kilograms of dry matter, with a named
destination for each. This course assesses **waste-stream recovery** from those
balances — food-waste dry matter, greywater volume and excreta nitrogen, each
routed to a productive use.

**Waste-stream recovery is deliberately not banded.** Every other indicator has a
per-site threshold from a reference design; this one does not, because the model
has no reference calculation behind it. Publishing a band without a reference
would be inventing a number, so it is marked from your mass balances against the
rubric instead. That asymmetry is honest, and it is on the method page too.

**Hot composting** is the pasteurisation route. The performance target is the
Australian composting standard as described by a state environment regulator:
core temperature above **55 °C for three consecutive days following each of at
least three turns**, and for manure, food and grease-trap feedstocks, **fifteen
consecutive days following each of at least five turns**. The standard also puts
the whole process at not less than six weeks.

Two scope notes you must carry. That is a **commercial windrow standard**, and no
household anywhere is bound by it — this course uses it as the target a bay is
designed against. And it is more demanding than it is usually quoted: fifteen days
after *each* of five turns, not fifteen days in total.

WHO's separate figure for composting excreta is **above 50 °C for at least one
week**, with the caution that composting "is mainly recommended as an off-site
secondary treatment at large scale".

**Anaerobic digestion** is strongly temperature dependent. Screening factors run
from 0.63 at Darwin to 0.07 at Canberra. And the biogas earns you nothing: it is
energy, it belongs to the proposed sequel, and only the digestate's nutrients
count here. Record it anyway. An unrecorded output is the same mistake as an
unrecorded import.

## Worked example: Alice Springs, where the heat does the work

Alice Springs has 90 days a year at or above 35 °C and an annual mean of 21.0 °C.
That is a liability for the garden and an asset here: reaching and holding 55 °C
in a compost bay is a question of managing moisture, not of adding heat.

Route the household's 380 kg:

| Stream | Mass | Destination | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Plant-based food waste | 228 kg/yr | Insect unit, then frass to soil | Week 4's substrate rule |
| Meat, dairy, cooked scraps | 152 kg/yr | Hot compost or digester | Excluded from larvae destined for fish by the course rule; also the feedstock class needing the 15-day pasteurisation |
| Spent mushroom substrate | ~5 kg per kg picked | Compost and soil | 1.46 % bioconversion by larvae makes the alternative arrow pointless (week 3) |
| Fish sludge | From the aquaponic system | Compost or digester | Vertebrate-derived; never to larvae under the course rule |
| Garden prunings | Site-dependent | Mushroom substrate, then compost | The one stream that passes through two subsystems |

The dry-matter total in must equal the dry-matter total out, plus the respiration
loss you have stated as an assumption. If it does not, you have lost a stream.

Alice Springs' particular trap: moisture. A bay that dries out stops composting
and never reaches pasteurisation temperature, so the water that hot composting
consumes is a genuine line item on the site's most constrained resource. It is
easy to write a compost plan here that quietly needs water the site does not have.

## Five sites

| Site | Composting | Digestion factor | The stream that causes trouble |
|---|---|---|---|
| Alice Springs | Fastest of the set; heat is free | 0.45 | Moisture, competing with irrigation |
| Darwin | Fast, but wet-season saturation | **0.63** | Volume: the highest garden output means the highest prunings load |
| Brisbane | Year-round | 0.57 | **Odour and vermin next to neighbours.** A strata site has the least tolerance for a badly run bay |
| Adelaide | Good most of the year | 0.34 | Seasonal: the glut from week 6 arrives all at once |
| Canberra | Winter bays stall | **0.07** | Digestion is close to pointless nine months a year. Design for compost, not gas |

## What this week hands you

**A mass balance per stream**, in dry matter, with a destination for every
kilogram and a stated loss term. This is the input to the proposal's waste-stream
recovery assessment, and the one place in the course where showing your working is
literally the deliverable.
