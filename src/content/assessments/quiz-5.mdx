  question="No site in this course absorbs even half of the household's excreted nitrogen on its own garden. What follows for a design?"

<QuizQuestion
  n={2}
  question="Why does a Canberra household need roughly 75 % more composting-toilet chamber capacity than a Darwin household of the same size?"
  options={["Colder conditions slow composting, so more material accumulates at once", "WHO's storage guidance requires 1.5 to 2 years below 20 °C against over 1 year above it", "Canberra households produce more excreta per person", "Frost prevents the chamber being emptied for part of the year"]}
  correct={1}
  why="It is a storage-time requirement, not a rate-of-decomposition calculation. Canberra's annual mean of 13.1 °C puts it in the 1.5 to 2 year band; Darwin's 27.7 °C puts it in the over-one-year band. The capacity difference is climate-driven and has nothing to do with how well either design is reasoned, which is why it is built into the reference designs so the bands absorb it."
  wrong="Slower composting is a real effect and points the same direction, but the sizing rule is the pathogen die-off storage time, which is stated as a duration."
/>

<QuizQuestion
  n={3}
  question="The rule that untreated greywater must go subsurface, must never be stored and must exclude kitchen water is the law in how many of this course's five site jurisdictions?"
  options={["All five", "Three", "One", "None"]}
  correct={3}
  why="All three elements together are New South Wales law, and none of the five sites is in New South Wales. Queensland permits surface irrigation and puts kitchen water inside the legal definition; the ACT has no greywater-specific regulation at all; NT and SA mandate subsurface but allow 24 hours' holding. The course adopts the strictest version as a design rule and says plainly that it is not the law where you are."
  wrong="'All five' is what most people would answer before week 11, which is the entire point of running that session as a citation exercise."
/>

<QuizQuestion
  n={4}
  question="A design reports 97 % water closure. What must be reported beside it, and why?"
  options={["Total roof area, to show the figure is achievable", "Garden water satisfaction, because closure can be raised by not watering the garden", "The import volume, because closure is capped at 100 %", "The storage volume, because closure depends on capacity"]}
  correct={1}
  why="Water closure is on-site water used divided by all water used. Water never used never enters either term, so a design that leaves its garden dry can post a very high closure figure. Alice Springs' excellent reference design reaches 97 % closure with 16 % garden water satisfaction. Published alone, the first number is an achievement; published together, the pair is a description."
  wrong="Import volume is genuinely useful and is reported too, but it does not catch this particular way of scoring well by doing less."
/>

<QuizQuestion
  n={5}
  question="Which statement about the reference bands is correct?"
  options={["A band 4 design has demonstrated a high distinction", "The bands guarantee that students at different sites are graded equally", "They are performance evidence inside the marking criteria, and the stability testing behind them is published with its limits", "They replace the marking criteria for the quantitative parts of the capstone"]}
  correct={2}
  why="The bands are ordinal, built from three constructed reference designs rather than a population of simulations, and they inform the calculations and integration criteria. ## The mechanics

Sat in the week 12 tutorial, at the start of the session, on paper. Twenty
minutes, 5 multiple-choice questions, no calculators and no numeric working —
if a question can be answered by arithmetic, it is not on this quiz.

Worth **4 %**. Five quizzes at 4 % make up 20 % of the course, spread across weeks
3, 5, 8, 10 and 12 so that keeping up is worth more than catching up.

**This website does not collect or store any answers.** It is a static site with
no server behind it. The practice questions above reveal their own answers in the
page, for your use only; nothing you do here is recorded, marked or transmitted
anywhere, and the graded quiz happens on paper in the room.
