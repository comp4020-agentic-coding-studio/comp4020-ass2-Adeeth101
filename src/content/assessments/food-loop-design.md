## The brief

> Your client has asked what their home could do. Before anyone spends
> $30,000 finding out, design the twelve months of measurement that would tell
> you — what to measure, where, how, how often, how well, and what decision each
> reading is for.

This is a **plan**, not a design. You are not asked to choose a system, size a
tank or size a garden. You are asked the question that comes before all of those:
*what would I have to know, and what would it cost to find out?*

The judgement being tested is **whether every instrument on your list earns its
place**. A measurement that no decision depends on is money spent to fill a
spreadsheet. A decision with no measurement behind it is a guess wearing a
number. Both are visible immediately in this submission.

Read the [measurement primer](/method/measurement/) first. It covers the
instrument classes this course expects you to know about — rain and tank, soil
and shade, food and waste logging, and how to state an uncertainty — and it is
short.

## The story, plainly

The [Household Resilience Loan Programme](/method/) and Common Ground
Engineering are **fiction**, invented so the engineering has a client, a budget
and a deadline. Nobody applies for a real loan. Nobody waits a real year. You
submit this plan at the end of week 7; the story then jumps twelve months, and in
week 7's lecture the specialist team hands back what a measurement year at your
home found. **Those findings are the same for every student who took your home,
whatever your plan said** — so a weak plan here does not damage Assignment 2, and
a strong one does not privilege it.

Everything you calculate before that handover is a **scoping estimate** on
published figures and the course's practice data. It is not an observation of
this house, and your submission should never call it one.

## What you submit

A single document, plus your workbook.

1. **The client and the decision inventory.** Your home, its fixed constraints,
   and the decisions the eventual retrofit will turn on — written as decisions
   ("connect the house roof or not", "keep fish or not"), each with the
   alternatives actually available.
2. **A measurement register**, one row per measurement, with:
   *what* is measured, *which decision* it informs, *sensor or manual method*,
   *where* on the site, *units*, *frequency*, and *stated accuracy or resolution*.
   A row missing any of those is an incomplete row.
3. **A seasonal coverage statement.** Which months matter for which measurement
   and why. A soil-moisture record that misses your site's driest month has not
   measured the thing that decides the design.
4. **Calibration and drift.** For each instrument class: what you would check it
   against, how often, and what you would do when it disagrees.
5. **A missing-data plan.** What you do when a logger fails. Which readings are
   recoverable by interpolation and which are not, and how a gap is marked so
   that next year's reader knows it was a gap.
6. **Safety and access.** Roof and tank access, electrical work, anything on a
   ladder, anything involving effluent or a confined space. Say what is done by
   a licensed trade and what is not done at all.
7. **The costed programme**, within the **separate $1,500 investigation
   allowance**. Equipment and services are charged against it; your own hours are
   scheduled, not charged. Show the total, show the headroom, and say what you
   dropped and why.
8. **An assumption and source log.** Every figure you used to plan with, where it
   came from, and whether it is sourced, a course assumption or your own estimate.

Indicative length: 2,000 to 3,000 words excluding tables and the workbook.

## What this assignment does not ask for

State these as out of scope rather than attempting them:

- **No sized food system.** No grow-bed area, no stocking density, no substrate
  tonnage as a design commitment. If you use those calculations to argue that a
  measurement is worth taking, label them scoping estimates.
- **No costed retrofit.** The $30,000 implementation allowance is Assignment 2's
  constraint, not this one's.
- **No pipe sizes, no wiring, no structural detail.** Not in either assignment.
- **No claim that anything has been measured.** Nothing has. That is the point.

## What loses marks

- An instrument list with no decisions attached, or decisions with no instruments.
- A frequency with no reason — "daily" because daily sounds thorough.
- Accuracy stated as "accurate" or omitted.
- A programme that fits the allowance because the expensive items were quietly
  left out rather than named and rejected.
- Describing a scoping estimate as a finding.
- Twelve months of measurement that all happen in one season.
