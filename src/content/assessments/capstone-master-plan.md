## The brief

> Produce the master plan for your site: every subsystem sized, every stream
> balanced, both hard constraints demonstrated, and an honest account of what the
> design does not close and what it would take to close it.

This is the document a competent reader could check. Not a proposal, not a
brochure — a plan with its arithmetic attached and its uncertainty declared.

The judgement being tested is **integration under competition**. Water, area and
the daily energy allowance are shared, and every week sized its subsystem as
though it had first call on all three. Reconciling that is the work.

## What you submit

1. **The Food Loop Design, revised** against its feedback. Say what you changed
   and why; unchanged work with the feedback ignored is visible immediately.
2. **Water and waste systems integrated**: sourcing, treatment, storage, waste
   routing and sanitation, sized against each other and against the food half.
3. **All five banded indicators** — water closure, garden water satisfaction, food
   energy closure, protein closure and nutrient closure — with **water closure and
   garden water satisfaction reported together everywhere they appear**.
4. **Waste-stream mass balances** for food-waste dry matter, greywater volume and
   excreta nitrogen. These are **not banded**; the balances themselves are the
   evidence.
5. **The full energy-load list**, peak-month average daily figure with the month
   named, every counted load with power and duty cycle, uncertain loads at their
   **upper** estimate, omitted loads named with a range. Hard constraint: 5
   kWh/day.
6. **The full costing** from the published schedule including the 10 %
   contingency. Hard constraint: $30,000.
7. **A safety and compliance checklist.** For each rule: what this course
   requires, what your jurisdiction actually requires, the instrument you read,
   and where the two differ.
8. **A scaled CAD site layout**: setbacks, tank positions, catchment, irrigated
   area, the sanitation system and its separation distances.
9. **A dry-year analysis** using the supplied [synthetic dataset](/method/datasets/):
   what fails first, in which month, what you would change, and what it would cost.
10. **An uncertainty list**: what you did not model, what you were unsure of, and
    a range for each.

Indicative length: 5,000 to 7,000 words excluding tables, drawings and
spreadsheets.

## How performance bands are used

Each banded indicator places your design in band 1 to 4 against your own site's
reference thresholds. **Bands are performance evidence inside the criteria, never
an automatic grade**, and they are read as part of *Calculations* and *Loop
integration*.

What a band actually tells a marker is whether your numbers are plausible for your
site — a Darwin design reporting 4 % food energy closure and a Canberra design
reporting the same figure are not the same result, which is the entire reason the
thresholds are per-site rather than national.

Three things bands do not do. They do not compare you to other students. They do
not guarantee equal grading outcomes across sites; the stability testing behind
them is [published with its limits](/method/#how-stable-are-the-bands). And they
cannot be earned by leaving something out: a high water closure with a low garden
water satisfaction reads exactly as what it is.

## Hard constraints

These are pass or fail, and they are checked before the criteria are applied.

| Constraint | Limit | How it is judged |
|---|---|---|
| Process energy | 5 kWh/day, peak month, counted loads | Uncertain loads at their **upper** estimate. A load you omitted without naming it is a failure, not a saving |
| Budget | $30,000 of new spend | Priced from the published schedule, including the 10 % contingency. Existing site inventory is free |
| Safety and compliance | The course design rules | Each stated with its jurisdiction, and distinguished from what the law requires |

A design that fits the budget by $1,000 is not comfortably compliant. The
reference designs themselves clear it by $1,125 at Darwin, which is inside the
noise of a screening model priced from retail listings, and the course says so
rather than pretending otherwise.
