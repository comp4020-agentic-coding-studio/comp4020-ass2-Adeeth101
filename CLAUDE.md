# SLOP4761 — harness

Rules for any agent working on this course site. Each one exists because getting
it wrong would damage the course, not because it sounded tidy. `README.md`
documents the platform; this file is about the course.

## Identity

**SLOP4761, level 4, Semester 1 2027.** The last three digits belong to this
repo. `src/course-config.ts` is the only place the code, title, dates,
description and tags are written; `src/lib/calendar.ts` derives every teaching
date from `startDate`. Do not retype a course fact into a page — import it.
*Why:* eighteen pages carry the same handful of facts, and the cheapest way to
ship a contradiction is to write one of them twice.

**SLOP4762 — Designing the Closed-Loop Household II: Energy and Shelter** is a
**proposed follow-on course**, not a catalogue entry. Mention it on the home
page, prerequisites, week 1 and week 12, always with that status word and never
as a link. *Why:* inventing a live destination for a course that does not exist
is the kind of small fabrication that makes a reader distrust the costed parts.

The Slop University name, marks and palette are fixed. All artwork is drawn in
the page from course data — there are no photographs and no stock imagery.
*Why:* every photograph available here would be stock or invented, and neither
belongs beside a bill of materials.

## Evidence

**Every number on a student-facing page comes from
`src/data/course-model.json`**, generated by `research/model/export_site_data.py`.
Change a constant, re-run the model chain, re-export. *Why:* the bands, costs and
site parameters are all downstream of one screening model, and a page that
restates a figure will silently disagree with it the first time anything moves.

**Label every figure.** One of: *sourced* (with a key resolving in
`research/sources.md`), *course assumption*, *indicative cost*, *model estimate*,
or *synthetic data*. Synthetic datasets say so in their first line and in their
filename. *Why:* this is a screening model wearing engineering clothes. A reader
must be able to tell which numbers would survive contact with a real site.

**A safety or legal claim cannot be made true by labelling it a course
assumption.** Verify its jurisdiction and scope, or drop the operational claim.
`research/verification-log.md` records what was fetched and read. *Why:* four of
the five sites are in jurisdictions where the draft's greywater rule is not the
law, and publishing it as law would have been simply wrong.

**Say which jurisdiction a rule belongs to**, and distinguish a *course design
rule* from a *legal requirement*. Where the course is stricter than the law, say
so and say why. *Why:* the transferable skill is telling those two apart.

## Voice

Australian English. Concise and specific. No hype, no promotional copy, no
implementation detail on a student-facing page.

**No two weeks may share a shape filled with interchangeable prose.** Each week
carries a concrete engineering question, a named quantitative method with its
formula, a worked example with real numbers at that week's lead site, an activity
or dataset, and a tangible output that advances the student's own design. *Why:*
twelve weeks generated from one template is the single most visible failure mode
of a course site, and a reader spots it in about forty seconds.

Prefer a sentence to a label with a colon. Keep units on every quantity.

## Site coverage

Five presets: Canberra suburban, Alice Springs rural-residential, Brisbane
townhouse, Adelaide suburban, Darwin rural-residential. Parameters and inventory
are fixed in `research/model/presets.py` (set v4).

**Weeks 2–11 each carry a Five sites section naming all five.** Lead sites:
Canberra 2 and 11, Brisbane 3 and 8, Darwin 4 and 9, Adelaide 5 and 6, Alice
Springs 7 and 10. Weeks 1 and 12 integrate all five. *Why:* each site leads once
where its climate helps and once where it hurts, so no student's site is quietly
the easy one.

Two site facts the model cannot score must survive every edit: Canberra's even
rainfall and high chill, and the Brisbane townhouse's 130 m² of growing space and
its strata constraints. *Why:* the fairness screen shows those two sites are
easiest on nothing and hardest on nothing the model measures. Their real
character lives only in the prose.

## Scoring

Climate-normalised reference bands **inspired by** NatHERS, built from three
constructed reference designs — not the NatHERS method, which derives bands from
a population of simulations. Bands are ordinal, 1 to 4, and are **performance
evidence inside the rubric, never an automatic grade**.

**Water closure is never published without garden water satisfaction beside it.**
*Why:* a design reaches high water closure by leaving its garden unwatered, and
one number alone rewards that.

**Waste-stream recovery is not banded.** It is assessed from submitted mass
balances. *Why:* the model has no reference calculation for it, and a band
without a reference behind it is a made-up number.

Bands are evaluated on the model's unrounded values; published percentages are a
rounded display. Never let a rounded display become a threshold. Recompute and
republish the bands whenever any course constant, preset or reference recipe
changes.

## Safety

The published safety rules are **course design rules** with their sources and
jurisdictions stated. Specifically:

- Fermentation guidance is restricted to **one named, cited recipe with its own
  conditions**. pH 4.6 is the *Clostridium botulinum* boundary for sealed
  shelf-stable product, not a general guarantee for any vegetable ferment, and
  the site must not imply otherwise.
- Insect feed rules are a course rule. Australian restricted-animal-material and
  swill law binds pigs and ruminants, not a household's own fish.
- Greywater, excreta storage and composting rules name their jurisdiction and
  their instrument.

**Students design systems. They are not asked to build, operate, eat from or
drink from anything.** Keep every page on that side of the line.

## Budget and energy

$30,000 costing allowance, including a required 10 % contingency, priced only
from the published schedule. 5 kWh/day peak-month process energy, counted loads
only, with uncertain loads declared at their **upper** estimate.

Both are hard constraints and both must be shown to be satisfiable by the
excellent reference design at all five sites before any feasibility claim is
made. *Why:* they were not, until the reference designs were costed against the
full schedule — see `research/critique-log.md` iteration 5.

Never claim compliance from a superseded total.

## Scope

Part I stops at food, water and waste. Energy and shelter belong to the proposed
sequel: biogas and other energy outputs are recorded but not credited, and only
digestate nutrients count.

Publish site cards, reference-band tables, assumptions, the price schedule and
one worked cross-site comparison. **No interactive calculator** — the point is
that students compute, not that the site does.

Partial closure and unavoidable imports stay visible everywhere. *Why:* full food
self-sufficiency needs about 372 m² per adult. A course that hid that would be
selling something.

## Verification

Before any commit: `pnpm check`. Before calling the draft done:
`pnpm check:evidence`.

`spec/data-integrity.test.ts` is the starter's and does not change.
`spec/course-contract.test.ts` is this course's, and tests the built output and
the generated data rather than the source: identity, the twelve weeks and their
dates, assessment weights and quiz weeks, lead-site coverage, prerequisites and
sequel, band integrity, the paired water indicators, and the price schedule's
bases. Test contracts, not prose.

`PROCESS.md` and `reflections/` are the student's own words. An agent never
writes prose into either.
