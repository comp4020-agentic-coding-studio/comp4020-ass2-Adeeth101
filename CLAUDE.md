# SLOP4761 — harness

Rules for any agent working on this course site. Each one exists because getting
it wrong would damage the course, not because it sounded tidy. `README.md`
documents the platform; `research/course-design.md` is the design contract; this
file is about keeping the course true to it.

## Identity

**SLOP4761, level 4, Semester 1 2027.** The last three digits belong to this
repo. `src/course-config.ts` is the only place the code, title, dates,
description, tags and prerequisites are written; `src/lib/calendar.ts` derives
every teaching date from `startDate`. Import a course fact; never retype it.
*Why:* dozens of pages carry the same handful of facts, and the cheapest way to
ship a contradiction is to write one of them twice.

**SLOP4762 — Designing the Closed-Loop Household II: Energy and Shelter** is a
**proposed follow-on course**. Mention it on the home page, week 1 and week 12,
always with that status, never as a link. *Why:* a live destination for a course
that does not exist is a small fabrication that makes readers distrust the rest.

The Slop University name, marks and palette are fixed. Figures are drawn in the
page from course data. Photographs and generated illustrations are optional and
never carry information a student needs.

## The case is fiction; the engineering is not

The **Household Resilience Loan Programme**, **Common Ground Engineering**, the
five families, their homes and the specialists are **teaching fiction**. Say so
once, plainly, on the programme page, the clients index and each Release B memo,
and not on every paragraph. Never invent real policy, lender endorsements,
links or people. *Why:* the fiction gives the engineering a client; it must not
be mistaken for a real scheme.

Formulas, yields, prices, biological facts and legal or safety rules are
**teaching claims** and need a source key that resolves in
`research/sources.md`, cited near the claim and shown on the page with a
readable reference. A student must never have to open the repository to find a
citation. *Why:* case facts need consistency; teaching facts need evidence.

The A2 deliverable is a technical annex and executive recommendation for a loan
application, **not** an approval and **not** a construction-certified design.
No income underwriting, interest rates, credit scores or financial advice.

## Five fixed homes, no others

Canberra (Taylor), Alice Springs (Nguyen), Brisbane (Patel), Adelaide (Rossi),
Darwin (Williams). **There is no custom-site option anywhere**, in pages,
assessment, decks or policies. No substituted land, no enlarged roofs or plots,
no new catchment. *Why:* equal dossier completeness is what makes the marking
comparable.

Every plan is computed from `research/model/case.py`. Areas are derived from
geometry and must reproduce the preset parameters within the larger of 15 m² or
4 %; run `python case.py` after any geometry change. Footprint, roof plan area
and roof surface area are three different quantities; never let a page treat
them as one. Brisbane's 40 m² shared-garden allocation is **common property
outside the 300 m² lot** and is never added to it.

Every dossier fact is **known**, **estimate** or **unknown**. Unverified service
routes and ground conditions are drawn as unknown, not with survey certainty.

## Two releases per home

**Release A** (intake, week 1) must not contain measured results. **Release B**
(specialist findings, week 7) is the same for every student who chooses that
home, whatever their A1 said. Its measured year is **synthetic**, generated by
`research/model/release_b.py` from the home's existing condition, and every page
and file keeps three things apart: sourced long-term BoM climate, the synthetic
measured year, and the separate synthetic dry year. One measured year does not
establish drought reliability. *Why:* A2 must be completable whatever the
quality of A1, and synthetic data must never pass for measurement.

Specialists analyse; they never choose the system.

## The design problem

Seven decision variables with published discrete ranges. Students find the
**lowest-cost option that meets the client's priority service targets**, then
justify any extra spending for resilience or output, comparing the existing
condition with three alternatives in a small spreadsheet grid and marking
dominated options. No optimiser, calculus or programming is required.

Client targets are set from `research/model/scenario.py` so that a feasible
option exists with a real margin; the site publishes the targets and a
rounded-down margin, **never the configuration**. Re-run `export_cases.py`
whenever a target, price or model constant changes.

**Worked optimisation examples use the Wattle Street practice house**, with its
invented round-number data, never an assessed home. Lead-home examples in the
lectures demonstrate one method on one component; they do not choose a design.

The first draft's closure indicators and reference bands survive only as
**diagnostics**: never a target, never a grade, never a recipe to approach.
Water closure is never shown without garden water satisfaction beside it.

## Evidence

Every number on a student-facing page comes from a generated data file
(`homes.json`, `release-b.json`, `design.json`, `practice.json`,
`measurement.json`, `course-model.json`, `datasets.json`) or is a hand-checkable
arithmetic step shown on the page. Change a constant, re-run the model chain,
re-export. *Why:* a page that restates a figure will silently disagree with the
model the first time anything moves.

Label every figure: *sourced*, *course assumption*, *indicative cost*, *model
estimate*, *synthetic data*, *practice data* or *case data*. Synthetic files say
so in their first line and their filename.

A safety or legal claim cannot be made true by calling it a course assumption.
Verify its jurisdiction and scope (`research/verification-log.md`) or drop the
operational claim. Distinguish a course design rule from the law, and say where
the course is stricter.

Never write that a household "needs" a fixed area to feed itself. Ecology
Action's figures are method-specific estimates with stated diet, yield, soil and
skill assumptions (V11), taught as such in week 9.

## Voice

Australian English. Concise and specific. No hype. Story excerpts (client notes,
site-visit extracts, memos, review questions) are brief and do a job.

**No two weeks share a shape filled with interchangeable prose.** Each lecture
carries a concrete engineering question, a named method with its formula, a
worked example with real numbers at that week's lead home, a Five homes
comparison, and the assignment component the week produces.

Prefer a sentence to a label with a colon. Keep units on every quantity.

## Teaching sequence

`src/lib/weeks.ts` is the **single source** for the week order, each week's
stage, its lead home and what its tutorial produces. The home timeline, `/weeks/`,
every week overview and the week banner on lectures and tutorials all read it.
Dates, titles and weights are **not** in it: those come from the content
collections and `src/lib/calendar.ts`, and the pages join the two. Never write a
second week map in another module. *Why:* the course briefly carried two, and the
home page advertised a topic the lecture did not teach.

The order is: 1 meet the client and the evidence question; 2 soil, plants and
irrigation demand; 3 mushrooms; 4 insects; 5 aquaponics; 6 preservation and
assembling the measurement plan; 7 water sourcing and the specialist handover;
8 treatment and reuse; 9 storage and seasonal reliability; 10 waste recovery and
integrated balances; 11 sanitation, nutrient limits and choosing between options;
12 integration and the investment recommendation.

Weeks 1–6 work out how each candidate system behaves and what would have to be
measured, and feed A1. A1 is due Friday in week 7; teaching may preview the
findings earlier, but the fictional handover follows submission, when the story
jumps twelve months and Release B is handed over. Weeks 7–12 use those findings to select and
justify the retrofit, and feed A2. **Calculations before the handover are scoping
or practice estimates, never claims about measured results**, and the pages say
so. Five quizzes at 4 % in weeks 3, 5, 8, 10 and 12; ten tutorial completion
checkpoints at 2 % in weeks 2–11, held as one 20 % assessment record; A1 20 %;
A2 40 %; no oral assessment. Weeks 1 and 12 tutorials are ungraded.

**Lead homes:** Canberra 2 and 11; Brisbane 3 and 8; Darwin 4 and 9; Adelaide 5
and 6; Alice Springs 7 and 10 — each once where its climate helps and once where
it hurts. Weeks 1 and 12 integrate all five, and weeks 2–11 each carry a Five
homes comparison naming all five. A week overview names only its own lead home;
the five-way comparison belongs on the lecture page.

All twelve lectures have linked teaching decks. Each tutorial carries inputs,
actions, templates or examples, outputs, checks and next steps, plus a spec whose
lines a student can check unaided; extend a tutorial rather than replacing it.

## Safety

Students design systems. They are not asked to build, operate, eat from or
drink from anything, and pages specify performance requirements and
commissioning checks, not how to construct hazardous systems. The rules for
greywater, excreta and urine, compost pasteurisation, fermentation (one named
recipe), insect substrates, drinking-water treatment and fish legality are
course design rules with their jurisdictions.

## Budget, energy and measurement

$30,000 implementation allowance including a 10 % contingency, priced only from
the published schedule. 5 kWh/day peak-month process energy, summed month by
month, uncertain loads at their upper estimate, month named. A separate $1,500
measurement allowance for equipment and services; labour is scheduled in hours.
Never claim compliance from a superseded total.

## Scope

Part I stops at food, water and waste. Biogas is recorded and not credited;
only digestate nutrients count. No interactive calculator: students compute,
the site publishes.

## Illustrations

Supplied generated conceptual artwork lives under `src/assets/images/` and is
processed through Astro's image pipeline. Five home portraits appear on the
client cards and matching dossiers; the homepage and method page each have one
purpose-specific illustration. Captions identify conceptual artwork, and the
scaled SVG plans remain the authority for geometry. Keep
`research/asset-manifest.md` in step with every used and rejected supplied file.

## Verification

Before any commit: `mise exec -- pnpm check`. Before calling a draft done:
`pnpm check:evidence`, and the built site inspected at 1920×1080 and 390×844.

`spec/data-integrity.test.ts` is the starter's and does not change.
`spec/course-contract.test.ts` tests the built output and generated data, not
prose. Diagram readability, teaching quality and story balance are judged in
the browser, not by tests.

`PROCESS.md` and `reflections/` are the student's own words. An agent never
writes prose into either.


### Final navigation and effort adjustment

Public tutorial URLs use `/tutorials/`; the platform collection remains `sessions` and old URLs redirect. Week navigation links to the lecture; its opening button opens slides. Every lecture and tutorial ends with a return to its own week overview. Navigation group labels sit above their links. Weeks 1 and 2 have no assignment work (5 hours each); weeks 3?12 retain 8 hours each. The indicative total is 90 teaching hours plus 4 finalisation hours, 94 overall.
